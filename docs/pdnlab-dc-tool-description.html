PDNLab DC

An open-source solver for DC voltage drop in printed circuit boards. It turns the copper of a KiCad board into a finite-element resistor network and reports voltage drops at selected load pins and modelled via currents for supported geometry.

Overview

PDNLab DC answers one question: when a board carries current, how much voltage is lost between the supply connector and each load pin, and how hard are the vias and copper working to carry it. It solves the steady-state DC case only. Frequency-dependent effects are out of scope.

What exists today working

  • A finite-element core for thin copper sheets, with lumped resistors and equipotential ties.
  • A via model with one rim node per contacted layer and a plated-barrel resistor between layers.
  • Self-checks for current, power and per-via current balance.
  • A Gmsh adapter that keeps Gmsh as an external program.

Prototype experiments/

  • A script that reads a saved KiCad 10 board, builds per-layer copper for one net, meshes it, solves it and reports pin drops and per-via currents.
  • Run on the +3V3 net of a 4-layer STM32H743 board.

Not built yet planned

  • Command-line tool and setup file.
  • General board importer and floating-copper pass.
  • Limit checks, reports, thermal coupling, viewer.
Validation status

The solver is checked against closed-form cases and against itself (conservation, mesh convergence). It has not been compared with a measurement or with another tool. Experiment 7, the cross-check against padne and KiPI, is blocked on installing them.

The problem it solves

Copper is not a perfect conductor. A rail that leaves a connector at 3.3 V arrives at each IC slightly lower, because current flows through planes, traces, thermal-relief spokes and vias that each have resistance. The loss is small on a good board and large on a bad one, and the difference is geometry that is hard to judge by eye.

A hand formula covers a straight strip. It does not cover a plane with cut-outs, a pad fed through three narrow spokes, or a rail that changes layers through an array of vias where some vias carry several times the current of others.

How the tool gets from copper to numbers

  1. Read the board. Tracks, pads, saved zone fills, vias and the stackup are taken from the KiCad 10 file.
  2. Build copper per layer. For the chosen net, shapes are merged into one polygon set per copper layer, with holes cut for vias and through-hole pads.
  3. Mesh. Each layer is divided into triangles by Gmsh, finer near holes and pads.
  4. Assemble. Every triangle contributes to the conductance matrix. Via rims and pads are tied into single electrical nodes. Plated barrels join layers as resistors.
  5. Solve. Supply pins are held at 0 V drop, a current is drawn at each load pin, and the sparse system gives every node voltage.
  6. Check and report. The real-board scripts report current balance, pin drops, via currents and current densities. Independent power balance is checked in experiments 1–5; it is not yet enforced for the real-board solve.

A worked case

On the +3V3 net of the test board, 100 mA drawn at each of 11 IC supply pins is 1.1 A in total. Fed from connector J4, the worst pin sits 3.025 mV below the supply. The vias are the interesting part: of 28 vias on the net, 16 carry current, and the busiest carries 337.6 mA. Its calculated local current density is higher at the layer’s hole edge than in the barrel wall. This does not establish a safe current limit.

Architecture

The planned architecture uses CAD-specific importers feeding a neutral board model and shared solver, with a results bundle feeding reports and a viewer. Today the prototype uses Python dictionaries and experiment-specific outputs; the general importer and stable results-bundle interface are not built yet.

From board file to copper geometry

The prototype reads the saved .kicad_pcb as an S-expression tree and extracts everything that belongs to one net. It does not use the pcbnew bindings. Only supported features of the chosen net are represented; this is not a complete board importer.

Building one layer

  1. Union all tracks, zone fills and SMD pads of the net on that layer.
  2. Add through-hole pads. On outer layers they are always added. On inner layers a pad is added only if it overlaps existing copper.
  3. Find the vias that touch this layer's copper: annulus overlap greater than 10-6 mm2. Add each via's annular ring to the copper.
  4. Cut a hole of radius ro = drill/2 + plating for every via and through-hole pad, drawn as an n-sided polygon.
  5. Simplify the outline with a 2 µm tolerance and keep the resulting polygons. In the refined path, simplification happens before holes are cut so their vertices are preserved; the original path simplifies after cutting. Pads that feed copper beyond their own outline are recorded for local mesh refinement.

The importer stage also exists as an audit. ipc2581_inspect.py and ipc2581_geometry.py compare saved board data with the KiCad 10 IPC-2581 revision C export, to test whether that export carries enough data to be the importer. On the 4-layer test board, tracks matched 785 of 785, pad entries matched 1,206 of 1,206, and pad holes matched 15 of 15. Pad entries are compared by identity, centre and axis-aligned extent; corner radii and full outlines are not verified. The audit reports track arcs, custom pads, slots and blind or buried vias as unsupported. These safeguards do not apply to the separate real-net parser, which currently ignores track arcs and via spans. The audit’s geometry gates affect the command exit status only with --strict.

Physics and finite-element method

At DC, a board is a stack of thin resistive sheets joined by via barrels. Each sheet is solved in 2D. Vias and parts are lumped resistors in the same matrix. A convenient picture is a very large resistor network: each triangle contributes a three-node conductance matrix, assembled much like circuit stamping in LTspice. The edge-resistor analogy has a limit: an obtuse triangle can have a negative cotangent edge weight, so individual element edges need not represent positive physical resistors.

The equation for one sheet

governing equation ∇·( g ∇φ ) = 0,    g = σ t = tρ

φ voltage, σ copper conductivity, ρ resistivity, t layer thickness, g sheet conductance in siemens. Sheet resistance is 1/g in ohms per square.

current density and heat J = −σ ∇φ,    q = g |∇φ|2

q is the Joule heat per unit area. The solver integrates it per triangle for the power check.

With the setup values used so far (ρ = 1.724 × 10-8 Ω·m), 35 µm outer copper has a sheet resistance of 0.493 mΩ per square, and 15.2 µm inner copper has 1.134 mΩ per square.

Linear triangle elements

One voltage per mesh node, linear interpolation inside each triangle. For a triangle with area A and edge vectors ei opposite each corner, the element matrix is:

element matrix Kij = g A ∇Ni·∇Nj = g ei · ej4 A

This is what fem.assemble_sheet computes for all triangles at once with a single einsum, then scatters into a sparse matrix. The result G satisfies G·v = current each node receives from outside the mesh, in amperes. The assembler rejects zero-area triangles, non-finite coordinates and non-positive conductance.

Merging nodes keeps the geometry untouched. Only the electrical unknowns are combined, so a via rim on one layer and a via rim on another layer must stay separate groups.

Via and pin model

A via is where a naive model goes wrong in two ways. Injecting the current at a single mesh node makes the computed drop depend on mesh size. Using one node for all layers shorts out the barrel. The model avoids both.

Rule 1: one rim node per contacted layer

On each layer the via touches, every mesh node on the hole rim is tied to one rim node. Rim nodes of successive layers are joined by a barrel resistor. A via that passes a layer without touching its copper adds no node there. The assembler stops with an error if two rims merge, because the barrel resistance would silently vanish, and it checks that each rim is a single closed loop of mesh-boundary edges.

Rule 2: the barrel is a thin tube

barrel resistance Rvia = ρ Lπ ( ro2 − ri2 )

L distance between the centres of two successive contacted layers, from the stackup. ro outer radius of the copper barrel, ri inner radius. The plating thickness is ro − ri. The code factors the difference of squares to keep precision for thin plating.

A board file stores one hole diameter d, and it can mean two things. The default is the finished hole after plating. The setup will state which applies.

Meaning of driro
Finished hole, after plating (default)d/2d/2 + plating
Drill, before platingd/2 − platingd/2

For a 0.3 mm finished hole with 25 µm plating the copper cross-section is 0.0255 mm2, which gives about 0.68 mΩ per millimetre of barrel at ρ = 1.724 × 10-8 Ω·m. A 0.2 mm hole has 0.0177 mm2 and about 0.98 mΩ per millimetre. The two hole readings differ by about 18 % in barrel resistance for the 0.3 mm case. The hole cut in each layer's mesh uses ro, where the sheet copper meets the barrel.

The barrel is ideal and uniform. It excludes contact resistance and 3D current spreading, which is why a 3D coupon comparison is a planned validation level.

Meshing

Gmsh is run as a separate program. The solver writes a .geo file per layer, calls gmsh <file> -2 -format msh4, and reads the MSH 4.1 result with meshio. Gmsh is never imported. Each layer meshes in its own process and the version string is recorded with every result.

What the geometry file contains

  • One plane surface per copper polygon, with every hole as an inner loop. Point sizes follow edge length, clipped between a minimum and maximum size.
  • An interior point from each pad’s overlap with the copper is embedded into a surface, using the centre where possible. Additional points sample pad outlines; pad boundaries are not fully conforming mesh edges. Missing pin nodes are reported.
  • A background size field for local refinement, described below.
  • Settings: linear elements, Frontal-Delaunay (Algorithm = 6), size taken from points and extended from the boundary.

On the simple shapes used for validation, terminals are named physical groups, and read_sheet_mesh finds them by tag rather than by comparing coordinates.

Local refinement

Current crowds at hole edges, so the mesh must be fine there. A Gmsh Distance field plus a Threshold field gives edge size at the hole edge and grows linearly to the global size over a ramp. Gmsh takes the smaller of this field and the point sizes everywhere.

WhereRule
Via holeDrawn as an n-sided polygon. Element size at the edge is one polygon side. It grows 0.15 to 0.3 mm per mm up to the global size.
Through-hole pad feeding copperSize 2πr/n, held out to the pad circumradius plus 1.25 times the zone's thermal gap, then the same growth. A pad that is its own island is skipped.
Global size0.5 mm in the reported run, with 96 sides per via hole; through-hole pad holes remain 24-sided

Refinement matters. Thermal-relief spokes 0.5 mm wide were covered by one or two elements on the first mesh. Resolving them moved the J1 and J5 results by 1.4 % to 1.9 %.

Finding the rim again

After meshing, the rim of a via on a layer is the set of mesh nodes at a distance from the via centre between 0.985 and 1.001 times the cut radius. At least three nodes are required, and they must form one closed loop of boundary edges. Polygon vertices lie on the circle with edges just inside it, so the window is deliberately asymmetric.

Linear solve

Two separate questions decide how the system is solved.

Is it solvable at all?

Every connected piece of the network must contain at least one fixed-voltage node. Otherwise the matrix is singular, and no solver fixes that. solve_fixed_voltages runs a connected-components pass first and raises an error that names the unanchored nodes. The planned board-level pass will go further: a piece with no source or sink is left out and reported as floating, and a piece with a source or sink but no fixed voltage stops the run and names the nets.

Which kind of matrix is it?

Sources in the networkMatrixSolverUsed
Fixed voltages to the reference, current sinks, resistors, rim ties applied by merging nodesSymmetric positive definiteSparse direct for small boards; conjugate gradient with algebraic multigrid for large onesdirect today
Ideal two-terminal source between two unfixed nodesSymmetric indefiniteSparse LUbacklog
Regulator with remote senseNonsymmetricSparse LUbacklog

The MVP uses only the first row: one reference per network, each source's return terminal at that reference. Direct factorisation memory grows quickly on large boards, so multigrid is planned from the start. Another tool was reported killed on a 220 by 200 mm board with 8 GB of memory. The cause there is not confirmed.

The transfer matrix

For each candidate supply connector the prototype does not assume load currents. It factorises the system once with scipy.sparse.linalg.splu and solves for a 1 A draw at each load pin. The result is a matrix Z.

transfer matrix Zpk = voltage drop at load pin p per ampere drawn at load pin k,   Δv = Z i

Any load pattern is then a matrix-vector product. The diagonal is the self-impedance of a pin. The full assembled conductance matrix is symmetric positive semidefinite. After fixing a reference in every solved component, the free-node matrix is positive definite and its load-pin transfer matrix Z is symmetric. If several pin labels share one electrical node, Z can itself be singular. The run records the symmetry error and the smallest eigenvalue as a built-in sanity check.

Closed-form references

strip, length L, width W Rstrip = ρ LW t  =  Lg W
annulus, radii a < b Rring = ρ2 π t ln ba
off-centre hole, offset d R = ρ2 π t arcosh a2 + b2 − d22 a b

Linear triangles represent a uniform field exactly, so the plain strip must agree to rounding error, and any larger error is a bug. The strip and the ring are kept as separate tests from the via geometry on purpose: a hole changes the resistance of a strip, so a strip with a hole cannot be judged against the plain strip formula. The off-centre case was added because the first ring cases are axisymmetric and would not expose current crowding on one side of a hole. A fourth test, a via joining two strips on different layers, uses a closed form built from the method of images with a conducting-hole correction.

Conservation gates for release 0.1

Each tolerance has a relative part and an absolute floor, so a quantity near zero cannot pass or fail by accident. The values are proposed starting points. For iterative solves the relative part becomes ten times the solver tolerance if that is larger.

GateCheckPasses when
1 AnalyticEvery analytic testEach meets its own relative-error limit

4 Power balancePower delivered at the terminals against copper loss plus lumped loss|difference| ≤ 1 nW + 10-6 × power delivered

The sheet loss is integrated from triangle voltage gradients, not computed as vT G v, and lumped losses come from branch voltage differences. That makes the power check independent of matrix assembly. The current and rim checks, by contrast, are residuals of the same matrix that was solved. They catch bookkeeping errors, not a wrong model.

Gate 6 is a cross-check and not proof of accuracy, because the reference tool is not known to be validated either.

Per-via currents, J4 supply

  • 28 vias on the net. 16 carry current, 12 end on passive pads and carry none.
  • Largest currents: v27 337.6 mA, v3 315.7 mA, v13 276.5 mA, v18 170.3 mA. Do not sum via magnitudes to obtain source current: one current path can cross several vias. The sum of each via’s maximum absolute barrel-segment current is 2.233 A. Conservation is checked using signed barrel currents across each layer boundary and the current carried by ideal through-hole leads; the source supplies 1.100 A.
  • Highest density, on v27: barrel 13.2 A/mm2, hole edge on In2.Cu 20.2 A/mm2 on average, and 38.2 A/mm2 at the local peak on B.Cu. These are modelled current densities, not validated current ratings or evidence of a thermal bottleneck.
  • Every rim balances to about 10-12 A.

Mesh convergence

Six mesh levels were solved: the first-run mesh, then h = 0.5 mm with 24, 48 and 96 sides per via hole, then h = 0.25 and 0.125 mm at 96 sides. Per-via current changes by at most 0.7 mA (0.2 %) between consecutive refined levels. Maximum drop and self-impedance change by at most 0.37 %. All 42 convergence and conservation gates pass.

Known limits and assumptions

Model

  • Steady state, DC, one board. Temperature does not yet change resistivity.
  • The barrel is an ideal tube. No contact resistance or 3D spreading.
  • Through-hole pins are ideal leads between layers.
  • Per-via post-processing rejects via-in-pad cases because rim and pad currents cannot be separated. The main transfer-matrix script does not apply that rejection to every solve.
  • Peak hole-edge density assumes an equipotential rim. It is nonzero even for a via with no net current.

Geometry and input

  • Refinement covers via holes and through-hole pads. SMD pads joined to a zone by spokes are not refined. The test net has none.
  • Only the zone's thermal_gap is read, not per-pad overrides.
  • Saved zone fills are taken as current.
  • Oval holes on selected-net pads are rejected. Track arcs are silently omitted by the real-net parser; via layer spans are read but ignored during contact construction. The current test net has no arcs and uses through vias. Other boards need explicit checks before solving.
  • The export audit is evidence for one board and one exporter version.

Trust

  • Not compared with measurement or with another tool. The reported net drop excludes return-copper resistance and component or connector contact resistance.
  • The transfer-matrix step excludes unreachable copper, but solve_scenario() still solves the full matrix and fails on unanchored floating islands.
  • exp08_real_net.py can exit successfully with unreachable loads, empty convergence comparisons or failed convergence flags. Review its JSON manually. The separate via-current runner does fail its own conservation and convergence gates.
  • The real-board runner does not yet enforce the independent power-balance gate. Its per-via tolerance uses total source current, whereas the planned release gate scales with local branch current.
  • Fabrication tolerance on finished copper thickness cannot be told apart from model error without calibration strips on a test board.
  • There is no thermal-vacuum chamber, so a vacuum mode could only be checked against analytic and 3D cases and would be labelled unmeasured.

The generic net script reports the matrix Z for each supply connector without selecting operating load currents. The via-current runner is specific to this STM32H743 board: it hard-codes +3V3, connector pin names and 100 mA at each IC supply pin, and its runtime depends on the mesh and computer. Defaults are --rho 1.724e-8 Ω·m at 20 °C and a plating thickness set in micrometres.

Roadmap

Seven phases, about 23 full-time working weeks for one developer, estimated. Phases 0 to 2 are committed: roughly 9 weeks or 360 hours. At 2 to 5 hours a week that is about 1.4 to 3.5 years, so phase 0 is split into small experiments with a reassessment after each. Each phase ends at a gate, and the next does not start until the criterion is met.

Electro-thermal coupling, planned

A second solve runs on a uniform grid over the whole board, starting at about 0.5 mm cells. Temperature is smooth, so it needs the full board but not a fine mesh. Each cell gets in-plane conductivity from its copper fraction per layer and through-plane conductivity from dielectric and via barrels. Heat sources are Joule heat from the DC solve plus component dissipation from the setup file. Air mode uses convection and radiation on both faces. Vacuum mode uses radiation and fixed-temperature mounting points only, which is the case that matters for space hardware.

temperature-dependent copper ρ(T) = ρ20 [ 1 + α ( T − 20 °C ) ],   α ≈ 0.0039 K−1

Where PDNLab DC aims to differ

  • Accuracy tied to a reference or a measurement, especially for the via and pad contact models.
  • Electro-thermal coupling with a vacuum mode.
  • Constraint-driven sign-off in CI: pin limits, via limits, current-density limits, automatic report.
  • A permissive licence and vendor-neutral input.
  • Memory and runtime on large boards.

padne is funded and active and uses the same core method. Differentiation, not duplication, is the stated risk mitigation.
